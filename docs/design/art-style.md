# Art style

## Direction

The Plutonian is an alien, dark and mysterious exploration RPG on a fictional Pluto.
Its world should feel cold and ancient, but inhabited: small settlements and working equipment offer points of warmth within a much larger, unfamiliar landscape.
Favor an otherworldly sense of discovery over realistic space hardware or horror spectacle.
Let a place suggest a history without explaining every detail.

## Shape and construction

Use stylized low-poly geometry with visible, deliberate facets and clear silhouettes.
Build forms from broad planes and simple volumes, adding detail where it explains construction, scale, or function.
Avoid fine surface noise and photorealistic ornament that disappear at the normal third-person camera distance.
Keep characters, creatures, and interactable objects recognizable against their surroundings.

Contrast angular terrain and crystalline features with the more ordered shapes of inhabited structures.
Nivalis uses compact, sheltered buildings with simple framed openings; the Old Relay uses monumental, regular architectural forms and repeated luminous lines.
Give each location a recognizable silhouette and a small family of repeated shapes, while leaving enough variation to suggest use and age.

Equipment and vehicles should look practical in this setting.
Use plates, frames, fasteners, fabric-like coverings, stone and metal-like materials as appropriate to their purpose.
The crossing wagon's framed cover, paneled body and heavy wheels are a useful reference for a constructed, repairable object.
Avoid introducing ordinary wooden construction that feels out of place on Pluto.

## Color, materials and light

Build the world around subdued cold blues, blue-gray, near-black and muted stone tones.
Use restrained warm amber light for inhabited spaces and machinery, and cyan light for waystones and other luminous features.
These accents provide landmarks and contrast against the dark environment; keep them concentrated so that they retain their meaning and do not wash out nearby shapes.
The relay's cyan bars and warm mechanical lights, and Nivalis's warm door lights against the blue surroundings, are existing examples.

Favor simple, mostly matte material regions and readable value changes across facets.
Suggest wear through construction and selective color variation rather than covering every surface with scratches or texture.
Darkness should create atmosphere while preserving enough separation to read paths, obstacles, characters and gameplay cues.
Judge colors under the actual in-game lighting, not only in a neutral modeling viewport.

## Characters and creatures

Keep the Plutonians' compact, faceted proportions and prominent silhouettes legible from behind, where players see them most often.
Gear should fit the body and remain identifiable by its large shapes and material blocks during movement.
For creatures and alien growth, use distinctive outlines and repeated anatomical or fungal forms that belong in the same faceted world.
Make threats readable before adding small details, and preserve contrast between their forms and the terrain.

## Applying the style

Use the existing Blender sources in `art/` and their corresponding runtime models in `assets/models/` as references.
Compare new work with the outpost, Old Relay, Plutonian, crossing wagon and nearby creatures in their intended scenes.
Check the silhouette at gameplay distance, material separation in shadow, and the prominence of any glow.
For animated or wearable assets, also inspect the shape in motion and where it attaches to the character.
Follow the [Blender workflow](../contributing/blender_workflow.md) for exporting and validating finished assets.
