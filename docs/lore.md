# The Plutonian

## The long dark

You are a Plutonian returning to Nivalis, a handful of inhabited rooms on a shelf of blue ice. The beacon should be the first thing you see from the crossing. Tonight you recognize home by its absence. Charon hangs above the roofs, pale enough to show where the tower ought to be.

On Pluto, sound can travel farther through the ice than it does through the air. Sometimes it comes out late. A dropped tool can strike the floor of a house a second time after everyone has gone to bed. On the deep shelf, a traveler can stop and feel another three or four steps pass under their feet. Children learn to wait out those extra steps before they try to count how many people are coming.

The long dark is what Plutonians call the stretch when they can no longer read the seams in the ice by the sky. Families time their crossings by the beacon during it. A voice from the shelf may be hours old by the time it reaches a doorstep. The turning light tells the people still out there where someone is waiting now.

## The people of Nivalis

Plutonians are small, upright people with broad dark eyes and sensory horns. The horns pick up the tremor of a loose floor plate and the pressure of fresh ice flexing underfoot. When two people have to wait in the dark, they sometimes rest their horns together. A familiar pulse is enough to tell who is there. Even reinforced helmets leave the horns clear. The luminous marks on the heavy helmets let a crossing party count heads when the ice starts repeating their steps.

Nivalis's squat habitats show an amber slit of light at each door. Households pool their methane pouches at a shared stove and salvage sheet metal to patch the runners on their sledges. A sealed battery gives off enough warmth to keep a latch from freezing shut. Most meals begin with what grows in the warm seams behind the houses. Ice worm meat goes into a pot that stays on the stove through the crossing season. Relay roach meat is usually carried home with the shell attached so the flesh stays in one piece on the walk back.

The field spear and trident are kept near the courtyard practice dummy. Children learn to test the ice with a pole there before they are trusted with a point. Along the trail, ice worms gather where the shelf carries the weight of passing feet. An old crossing story tells of a worm that kept striking at the same empty sledge track until its teeth wore flat. Down in the Old Relay, roaches shelter in the seams around the amber lamps. A keeper inspecting the lamps used to tap each housing and wait for the legs inside to settle.

## The Last Light

Nemi keeps the beacon and tends the courtyard while families are out on the ice. Before a crossing they put one washer on the tower sill for each person expected home. On a good night the sill is bare by the time they go inside. When you arrive, the tower is dark and there are washers left on it. Nemi asks you to bring back the spare core before the long dark sets in.

A charged spare core waits north of the outpost, on a stone plinth in the blue crystal cavern. The keepers have used that cavern for as long as Nemi can remember. A core brought there cold will eventually grow warm enough to hold against bare palms. Nemi knows how to tell when one is ready by the pulse of its light. In older households, people say the crystals have been giving back the warmth of the first stove a little at a time. Nobody in Nivalis remembers the stove they mean.

The blue waystones stand in pairs from Nivalis to the cavern. In blowing snow a traveler waits between the stones until both lights of the next pair come into view. The parents of a child making their first crossing teach them to stop at every pair and look back. The trail should make sense in both directions before they go on.

Nemi can fit the charged core into the tower and restore the signal. If you attack them, they flee. If they survive, they return to the courtyard once they feel safe and refuse to help you. The socket on the south side of the tower still takes the core. The families on the ice see the same light come on.

## The Old Relay

The oldest room in Nivalis lies under the ice. The first survey crews worked out of that room before the outpost had a name. They placed lamps on the shelf to find their way home and kept a wall map of crossings they had walked. The first survey log begins with the room and the decision to keep a light burning above it. The crews' own journey to Nivalis remains a subject of argument around the stove.

The beacon also supplies power to the Old Relay. When the tower goes dark, the round hatch northwest of it seals. Once the beacon is lit again, the hatch opens onto the old vestibule. Amber floor lamps lead to the maintenance switch. Beyond the gate it raises, the survey log still sits on its pedestal in front of the wall map.

Old survey maps marked the places where the ice gave back footsteps. Some marks have dates beside them. Others have been retraced so often that the original dates are buried. One mark sits under the modern courtyard, almost directly below the sill where Nemi leaves the washers.

The line the keepers still know by heart comes from *The First Watch*.

> There is shelter here, and someone is waiting.
